Atlas of the New World

Seville · 10 Mar – 30 Jun 2026

When Europeans arrived in America, they had to invent a new way of representing the world. This exhibition brings together the first maps of the American continent: from the initial sketches full of uncertainty to the detailed atlases of the 17th century.

1492

First Landing — Bahamas

On 12 October 1492, Columbus made landfall on an island he called San Salvador. His sketchy first map of the Caribbean coast was revolutionary — a new world was beginning to take shape on European charts.

1498

Terra Firma — Venezuela

On his third voyage, Columbus reached the mainland of South America near present-day Venezuela. He recognised he had found 'another world', a continent unknown in classical geography.

1507

America Named

German cartographer Martin Waldseemüller published the first map to use the name 'America', honouring Amerigo Vespucci. His 1507 world map showed a continent separate from Asia for the first time.

1519

Magellan Departs Seville

Ferdinand Magellan set sail from Seville in 1519 on humanity's first circumnavigation. His voyage would finally prove that the Americas were an entirely separate landmass and reveal the true scale of the Pacific Ocean.
